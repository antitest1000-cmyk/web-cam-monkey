What Peer-to-Peer Video Chat Means

Browser video chat often uses WebRTC. The term describes technology that lets browsers negotiate a real-time media connection, sometimes directly between the two devices.

The signaling step

The signaling step

Before video can flow, each browser needs connection details from the other. A signaling service helps exchange those setup messages and pairs visitors. It is like a switchboard for starting a call; it does not guarantee the media route will be direct.

The media path

The media path

WebRTC tries to send audio and video between the two browsers. Network address translation, firewalls, and other network conditions can affect which route works. Some systems may use a relay when direct connectivity is not possible; this site's current configuration may not support every network.

Peer-to-peer is not anonymity

Peer-to-peer is not anonymity

A direct connection can expose network information to the other participant or to infrastructure involved in connection setup. Your browser and hosting services may also process technical connection data. Do not assume that peer-to-peer means invisible or untraceable.

It is not a recording guarantee

It is not a recording guarantee

This site does not advertise a recording feature, but another participant can capture the screen or use a separate camera. WebRTC itself cannot prevent that. Treat every live conversation as something that could be recorded by the person on the other side.

Why video quality varies

Why video quality varies

Camera hardware, browser permissions, Wi-Fi strength, network congestion, and firewall rules all affect quality. If the connection does not start, check permissions and try a stable network. More details are in our FAQ.
